# 3 m Modular Boat — Joining and Material Research

## Reality check

A 3 m craft is a real boat, not an enlarged print. A glued chain of FDM hull sections is **not accepted as a seaworthy structure** without engineering, coupon tests, flotation/stability evidence, local legal review, and controlled test progression. ISO 12215-5:2019 covers hull design pressures and scantlings for monohull small craft up to 24 m; it is the relevant engineering benchmark, not a casual glue recipe. [ISO 12215-5:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/69552.html)

## Viable directions to investigate

1. **Printed tooling/moulds, composite hull:** printed pieces form the mould or internal tooling; a conventional engineered composite hull carries the load.
2. **Printed nonstructural fairing over a frame:** CNC-cut bulkheads/longitudinals and an engineered hull system carry loads; printed skins provide shape only.
3. **Modular printed test hull:** only for a tethered, unmanned, protected-water proof of assembly. It must have independent flotation compartments and an immediate recovery plan.

## Seam strategy: test, do not assume

- Use a long overlap flange or keyed scarf, with mechanical fasteners/compression as the primary assembly method.
- Use a compatible gasket or sealant as a water barrier, not as the only structural load path.
- Print, condition, and destructive-test seam coupons in the actual material/orientation before making hull sections.
- Test static leakage, cyclic flex, heat exposure, and post-soak strength before escalating size.
- Do not mix unknown recycled filament into structural or watertight seams.

## Material decision gate

Record the exact filament polymer and manufacturer. PETG/ASA may be candidates for prototype panels; PLA is unsuitable for sun/heat-exposed service. Bottle recycling is a separate materials project: sorting, washing, drying, extrusion consistency, and coupons come before hull use.

## Stop conditions

Any leak, seam craze, delamination, distortion, unplanned buoyancy loss, or instability blocks water use. No passengers, deep water, motor power, or remote operation in the first hull trials.
