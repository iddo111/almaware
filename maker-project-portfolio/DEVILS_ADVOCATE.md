# Devil's-Advocate Gate — Applies to Every Project

## Core challenge

The attractive version of these projects assumes that a good CAD model, several printers, and AI assistance make the result trustworthy. They do not. Printed-part anisotropy, unknown recycled material, leaks, electrical faults, software errors, and user misuse can defeat a concept that looks convincing.

## Non-negotiable gates

1. **One hazard at a time:** bench-test a mechanism before integrating power, water, autonomy, or a user.
2. **No human dependency:** the walking aid must not carry, arrest a fall, steer a person, or replace a certified mobility device. The submarine is uncrewed only.
3. **No open-water launch:** no boat/submersible release until buoyancy, stability, watertightness, retrieval, local requirements, and a responsible safety review are documented.
4. **No unreviewed machine motion:** CNC output is a reviewable plan, never automatic machine control. Operator confirms material, workholding, tool, zero, enclosure, and emergency stop.
5. **No material assumptions:** recycled bottle feedstock is UNKNOWN until sorted, cleaned, dried, extruded, and coupon-tested. It cannot be used for pressure, flotation, or mobility-critical parts by default.
6. **No magical AI claims:** assistants can summarize sources and prepare options; they do not validate designs, perform engineering sign-off, or guarantee safe settings.

## Kill criteria

Pause a project after any uncontrolled motion, water ingress, structural crack, overheating, unexpected gas/chemical behavior, control loss, or discrepancy between CAD and measured part. Preserve the artifact and record the failure; do not retry by increasing power, speed, pressure, or scale.
