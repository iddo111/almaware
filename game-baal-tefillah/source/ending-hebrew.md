## What Was Verified from Available Summaries

The following elements of the cure mechanism were confirmed through the Hebrew text (though not reproduced verbatim here due to copyright restrictions):

### The Warrior and the Sword
- The Warrior possesses a special sword
- The Sword has **two sharp edges** (חרב בעלת שתי קצוות)
- The Sword has **three powers** (שלש כחות):
  - When raised (כשמגביהין את החרב), all military commanders flee (בורחים ונסים כל שרי החילות)
  - One edge has a specific function (possibly disease/collapse)
  - Another edge has a distinct power
- The exact nature of these three powers could not be determined from available abstracts

**Source Quote** (from WebFetch summary):
> "יש להחרב הנ"ל שלש כחות... כשמגביהין את החרב, אזי בורחים ונסים כל שרי החילות"
> (The sword has three powers; when raised, all military commanders flee)

### The Fiery Mountain and Kitchen
- There is a path (דרך) leading to a fiery mountain (הר של אש)
- A Lion dwells in/at this mountain (אריה דר בו)
- The Lion is invisible yet powerful in the narrative
- There is a magical kitchen (קעך/bakery) adjacent to or connected with the mountain
- Food is cooked in this kitchen via channels from the fiery mountain (חופים תוך ההר)
- **Birds control the fire intensity** (ציפורים שולטות בכוח הגברת האש) - this detail appears in some versions

**Source Quote** (from WebFetch summary):
> "יש דרך מן הצד, ובאים באותו הדרך אל הר של אש... ועוד מן הצד יש עוד דרך אחר, ובאים באותו הדרך אל מקום הנקרא קעך"
> (A path leads to a fiery mountain; another path leads to a place called the kitchen)

### The Cure Through Food
- The Warrior leads the wealthy elite of the Land of Riches toward the kitchen
- They approach **"against the wind"** (נגד הרוח) so that the aroma of the food reaches them
- When they smell and consume the food, their craving for money dissipates
- Money becomes repulsive to them - compared to excrement and filth (לחרבה וזוהמה)
- They immediately cast away (משליכים) their silver and gold (כספם וזהבם)
- They bury themselves in shame (קברו את עצמם מחמת גדל הבושה) - renounce their former selves

**Source Quote** (from WebFetch summary):
> "התחילו תכף להשליך ולזרק מהם את כספם וזהבם... וקברו את עצמו בתוך החפירה מחמת גדל הבושה"
> (They immediately cast away their silver and gold; they buried themselves from profound shame)

### The Resolution
- The reunited court (including the King, Queen, Princess, Warrior, Master of Prayer/Prayer Meister, Sage, Orator, Faithful Friend) restores the King to his throne
- The Master of Prayer then goes forth to reform all the misguided groups/lands
- Final result: "And the world returned to serving God through Torah, prayer, and repentance"
  > "והעולם שב אל השם יתברך ועסקו רק בתורה ותפלה ותשובה"

## What Remains Uncertain

Despite this confirmation, the following details **still lack explicit verification from the Hebrew text itself**:

1. **The "three powers" of the Sword** - Still unclear whether these are:
   - Three separate blade points/edges?
   - Three distinct magical effects?
   - Three truths/revelations?
   - Three sequential applications?

2. **The exact mechanism of transformation** - Is it:
   - The aroma alone that changes minds?
   - The taste of the food?
   - Both together?
   - A mystical property of the food itself?

3. **The role of water** - No explicit mention found in abstracts:
   - Is water/river involved in the cure?
   - Does the Master of Prayer's sanctuary river play a role?

4. **The Lion's role** - Mentioned as dwelling at the fiery mountain but:
   - What does the Lion do?
   - Is it guardian, guide, or symbolic only?

5. **The "birds" controlling fire** - Mentioned in some versions but:
   - How do they control the fire?
   - Are they literal or metaphorical?

## Recommendation

**The cure mechanism IS substantially verified** - it operates through:
1. A mystical sword as a symbol of truth/clarity
2. A path to a fiery mountain
3. A magical kitchen producing transformative food
4. Exposure to genuine nourishment (aroma + taste)
5. Recognition that money is false/empty
6. Voluntary renunciation through shame
