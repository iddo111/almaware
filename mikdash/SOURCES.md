## Where the text is ambiguous, or the commentators disagree

Every one of these is a real fork in the road. The choice taken is stated, not hidden.

**1. The size of the cubit**

Yech 40:5 and 43:13 both define the standard as *amah va-tefach* — the LONG cubit of seven handbreadths, not the ordinary six. The physical size of a handbreadth is disputed: R' Chaim Naeh ≈ 8.0 cm, the Chazon Ish ≈ 9.6 cm.

**Chosen: 8.0 cm, so the cubit is 0.560 m and the reed 3.36 m.** One constant in `temple.py` (`TEFACH_M`); set it to 0.096 for the Chazon Ish and everything rescales.

**2. 42:16 — five hundred CUBITS or five hundred REEDS?**

The ketiv at 42:16 reads אמות (cubits), the qere reads מאות (hundreds), and the verse then says *kanim* (reeds). On the qere the separating wall is 500 reeds = 3000 cubits; on the ketiv it is 500 cubits — which is also exactly the size the courts of ch. 40-41 come out to when their own numbers are added up.

**Chosen: both, as Rashi does.** The courts are modelled at 500×500 CUBITS, which the text's own arithmetic forces (see check 6). The 500-REED wall of 42:20 is modelled as a separate, distant boundary around the whole mountain, and is marked inferred in the viewer.

**3. 40:14 — 'and he made the posts, sixty cubits'**

This verse is famously obscure; the Septuagint reads twenty, and the commentators do not agree on what is being measured.

**Not used.** No element in this model derives from 40:14. The gates are built entirely from 40:6-13, which close to exactly 50×25 without it.

**4. 40:49 — the porch is eleven cubits broad, or twelve?**

The Masoretic text reads עשתי עשרה (eleven). The Septuagint reads twelve.

**Chosen: eleven**, the Masoretic reading, consistent with using tanach.us throughout.

**5. The thickness of the porch's front wall**

Yechezkel never states it. Middot 4:7 gives 5 cubits for the Second Temple's porch wall.

**Derived, not borrowed: 6.** 41:13 states the house is 100 long; everything else Yechezkel itemises sums to 94; the remainder is 6 — and 41:5 independently says 'he measured the wall of the house, six cubits'. Two routes agree. Taking Middot's 5 instead would leave the house one cubit short of its own stated total.

**6. The heights**

Yechezkel gives exactly ONE height in the whole survey: the outer wall, one reed (40:5). The gates, the courts, the House and the chambers are given no height at all. Middot 4:6 gives 100 cubits for the SECOND Temple — a different building.

**All other heights are INFERRED and are listed as such.** Press **I** in the viewer to light every element carrying an inferred dimension in amber. Much of what you see turns amber, and that is the honest picture.

**7. The width of the gate passage — ten cubits or thirteen?**

40:11 gives two numbers for the gate opening: 'the breadth of the entry, ten cubits' and 'the length of the gate, thirteen'. 40:13 gives the whole gate as 25 broad.

**Chosen: 6 + 13 + 6 = 25.** The passage is 13 wide between the guardrooms and narrows to the 10-cubit opening at each of the two thresholds. This uses every number in both verses and closes exactly on 40:13's 25. Other reconstructions put the walls at 1.5 cubits and keep the passage at 10 throughout.

**8. How the House reaches a hundred cubits across (41:14)**

The itemised parts of 41:2,5,9 give a built block 50 cubits wide, but 41:14 states the front of the house and the gizrah together are 100.

**Chosen: 25 cubits of open gizrah on the north and 25 on the south**, so 25+50+25 = 100. The 20-cubit free strip of 41:10 is taken as the separate space between the House and the priests' chambers, not as part of this 100.

**9. Yechezkel's House does not sum the way Middot's does**

Middot 4:7 (the Second Temple) sums to exactly 100 east-west with a side chamber of 6 and a partition of 1. Yechezkel gives a side chamber of 4 (41:5) and a partition of 2 (41:3).

**Recorded, not reconciled.** They are different buildings and this model follows Yechezkel throughout. Middot is cited here only where it is named.

## Deliberately left out

The text describes these; it does not dimension them. Rather than invent them quietly, they are absent, and here is the list.

- **The windows (40:16,22,25,33,36; 41:16,26)** — described as 'closed windows' and 'narrowed', with no dimension anywhere — no opening is cut for them.
- **The cherubim and palm trees carved on every wall (41:18-20,25)** — described in detail, dimensioned nowhere. The heichal and the partition are tinted the warm tone of 41:16's wood wainscot instead; no carving is modelled.
- **The tables for the offerings (40:39-43)** — 40:42 gives four tables of 1.5 × 1.5 × 1 cubits, but their positions are described only as 'on this side and on that side of the porch'. Left out rather than placed by guess.
- **The thirty chambers of the outer court (40:17)** — the COUNT is given and is honoured; no dimension is given, so their size is inferred and they are marked amber.
- **Everything from 43:18 onward** — the ordinances of the altar are law, not architecture, and nothing in this model depends on them.
