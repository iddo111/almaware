2) From the web:  
A memristor is a nonlinear two-terminal device that relates charge and flux, first theorized by Leon Chua in 1971 [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memristor]. It exhibits memory of past voltages/currents via resistance changes, often implemented physically using materials like TiO₂ or SiO₂ [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memristor]. In neuromorphic computing, memristors act as artificial synapses: their conductance evolves based on signal history and timing, emulating synaptic plasticity for energy-efficient, analog learning [https://www.tutorialspoint.com/neuromorphic-computing/neuromorphic-computing-memristors.htm] [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuromorphic_computing]. Key advantages include non-volatility, analog storage, scalability, and in-memory computation—making them ideal for brain-inspired hardware [https://www.tutorialspoint.com/neuromorphic-computing/neuromorphic-computing-memristors.htm].
