# Internal Spiral-Propulsor Submarine — Concept Boundary

## Intent

Study a hollow-submarine concept with an **internal, shrouded spiral propulsor**. The propulsor is contained inside a water duct through the hull, so the external hull can remain smooth while water enters, is accelerated by the spiral rotor, and exits astern.

## Concept architecture

```text
inlet grille -> internal water duct -> spiral rotor / stator -> outlet nozzle
                         |                  |
                      hull shell       isolated drive motor
```

- The pressure hull is separate from the wet propulsion duct.
- The spiral blades are a mechanical impeller, not exposed battery electrodes.
- A non-conductive shroud, inlet grille, and outlet nozzle contain the rotor and protect occupants and wildlife.
- A sealed low-voltage motor and ordinary battery pack are the baseline energy system.

## Seawater-electrode idea: research only

Using seawater and blade-electrodes as a battery or propulsion source is **not a build-approved design**. Electrolysis can create hydrogen, oxygen, chlorine-containing gases, corrosion products, heat, and unpredictable electrical paths. It is especially unsuitable inside a sealed or crewed hull.

Any future exploration must remain a benchtop, ventilated, low-energy materials experiment with electrical isolation, corrosion testing, and a defined chemical-safety review. It must not be connected to a vehicle, enclosed volume, or human-carrying system.

## First safe project milestone

Create a dry, non-powered geometry study that defines:

1. Hull envelope and separate wet-duct volume.
2. Rotor diameter, clearance, intake/outlet area, and debris grille.
3. Shaftless magnetic-coupling versus sealed-shaft tradeoff.
4. Emergency water-drain and manual access path.
5. A non-operational scale mock-up acceptance check: rotor clears the shroud without contact and all wet/dry boundaries are visible.

## Blockers before any prototype

- Intended operating environment and scale.
- Whether this is an uncrewed model or a human-carrying vessel.
- Materials, buoyancy, pressure, propulsion power, and emergency-stop requirements.
- Electrical and chemical safety review for any seawater-electrode experiment.

No propulsion hardware, battery chemistry, electrode arrangement, or vehicle construction is authorized by this concept note.
