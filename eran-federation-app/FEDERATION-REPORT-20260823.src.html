What the field has proven — over the open internet

  • Petals (Yandex Research / BigScience) — BitTorrent for inference. Each volunteer hosts a few transformer blocks and forwards activations to the next peer. Runs Llama-2-70B and BLOOM-176B over the internet, up to 10× faster than offloading for interactive generation, with fault-tolerant routing and load balancing.
  • exo — a mesh over heterogeneous consumer hardware; the viral demos split Llama-3-405B across Macs, iPhones and Raspberry Pis.
  • Both are pipeline-parallel over the WAN — exactly the shape the DGX chain already runs internally, just with hostile latency.

The honest read for us: Petals-style inference is real, but it pays off when the model is far too big for one box. Iddo already owns the box. Where the federation genuinely wins is the opposite end: models so small that a free edge Worker computes them outright — which is precisely what tonight proved, and where nothing else in the workspace has ever been able to go.

3 · Distributed training / אימון מבוזר — the honest research

שורה תחתונה קודם: הענן החינמי לא יכול לאמן שום דבר רציני, וזה לא עניין של חוכמה או של תכנון — זו מגבלת חומרה וזמן. הטכנולוגיה לאימון מבוזר כן קיימת ועובדת, אבל היא רלוונטית לצי שלך ולמכונות שכורות, לא לשכבות החינמיות.

The technology that does work

  • DiLoCo (DeepMind) — local SGD with hundreds of inner steps between syncs. Matched the synchronous baseline on a 150M model while communicating 500× less.
  • OpenDiLoCo (Prime Intellect) — the open implementation. Trained across two continents and three countries at 90–95% compute utilisation, and scaled DiLoCo to 3× the original size.
  • DiLoCoX — extends this to clusters whose links are only hundreds of Mbps, which is the normal condition outside a datacentre.

Verdict. Free tiers: no. Your own fleet plus rented spot nodes: yes, and DiLoCo is the right shape for it — the fabric is 105 Gbit/s inside the house, so the interesting case is adding a machine that is not in the house without the sync killing you. That is precisely the problem DiLoCo solves.

And one thing the free tier can train: the ant farm. Each ant is tiny, CPU-only, and independent of every other ant — embarrassingly parallel, no gradient ever crosses the wire. See §5.

One giantSeveral small
You haveQwen3-Coder-480B, PP×3, 28 tok/s. gpt-oss-120b on eran4.Nothing yet as a swarm; the pieces exist.
StrengthMulti-step reasoning, whole-repo context, code correctness. Does not fall over on the hard task.Throughput and cost. Embarrassingly parallel across mechanical work.
The 2026 findingThe field converged on hybrid routing, not on either pure answer: a frontier reasoner scopes and plans, cheaper models execute, and the mix is deliberate. A Haiku-class small model reaches roughly 90% of the mid-tier's accuracy at a fraction of the cost — but small models degrade unpredictably exactly where it hurts: multi-step reasoning, code correctness, precise instruction following.
Proposal — "the giant plans, the small ones type." Keep the 480B on eran1–3 as planner and reviewer. Add a swarm of small coders for the mechanical half (renames, test scaffolds, doc passes, mechanical migrations). Do not benchmark it on SWE-bench — benchmark it on his own repo, on tasks already done, and keep it only if the swarm's output survives the giant's review more often than it wastes it. That measurement is the deliverable, not the swarm.

The two rooms, and which one to build first

🐜 חוות הנמלים / The Ant Farm

Why it fits the federation perfectly: each ant is a tiny organism, CPU-only, and independent. No gradient crosses the wire, so a visitor's browser tab, a Kaggle notebook and a $4 VPS can all run ants without any of the distributed-training problems in §3. A tournament is the natural shape.

What the visitor sees: the colony working, the reigning queen, and her record — with the losses shown too.

This is the one that pays for itself. It turns spectators into compute, honestly and with consent.

🧸 חדר התינוקות / The Nursery

Why it matters more: it is the room ERAN actually needs — the playroom design already written. A visitor could hear what he hears (the ear is already live in the cloud) and watch a percept land on a prototype.

The hard constraint: the nursery is where a visitor would teach him — and teaching is learning, and learning happens only at home (§6). So version one is a nursery with a one-way window: the world watches and listens; only the fleet raises him.

Later: visitor "taps" queue as proposals the fleet may accept — never direct writes.

Recommendation: both, in that order — ant farm first, nursery second. The ant farm is safe by construction and proves the federation can borrow compute at all. The nursery is the one with the soul in it, and it deserves to be built on a lane that has already been tested with something that cannot be hurt.

6 · The BRANCH law / חוק הענף

"הערן שירוץ שם יהיה ברנץ בכל מקרה, לא הראשי." — עידו, 2026-08-23

He is right, and it needs to be a mechanism, not a promise. Half of it is already in the code shipped tonight; the rest is named here so it is not forgotten.

RuleHow it is enforcedState
A cloud ERAN never learnsThere is no spawn, no Hebbian pull, no scar and no forgetting anywhere in brain.js or eran.js. The capability is absent, not disabled.shipped
A cloud ERAN never writes backThe only write path is the fleet pushing to the cloud with a Bearer token. There is no route that sends anything home.shipped
A branch cannot impersonate another earA snapshot whose dim differs from the cloud cochlea is refused 409 — a brain grown by an ear the Worker does not have would be a guess wearing his name.shipped
A branch says how old it isEvery answer carries age_s and snapshot_state; over 15 minutes it says stale.shipped
A branch carries its lineageAdd lineage: {parent_beat, parent_host, branched_at, branch_id} to the snapshot, and have every answer quote it. Then no screenshot of a cloud ERAN can ever be mistaken for the child.proposed, ~2 h
The main line is never a branch's targetThe organism's state directory takes writes from one process under a lock. A cloud branch has no credential for it and must never be given one.standing

8 · What I did not do, and why / מה לא עשיתי

  • I did not create any accounts. Signing up for services is yours to do, not mine — and the standing rule against mass free-tier signup ("gets you banned and sprays your PII") has not moved. What I did was use the Cloudflare account you already own.
  • I did not put anything from tier C into the cloud — mic capture, the Café Claude recordings, the held-out exam vault, לאור's Darwish corpus, headscans, WhatsApp. Those are a no on principle, not on engineering.
  • I did not train anything on free cloud, because §3 says it cannot be done honestly and the DGX is the trainer.
  • I did not give the cloud copy the ability to learn. Not disabled — absent.
